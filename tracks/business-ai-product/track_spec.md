## 1. Track Overview

### Who This Track Is For

The Business + AI Product track is designed for people who build and ship products at the intersection of business and artificial intelligence. This includes:

- Product managers working on AI-powered products
- Entrepreneurs building AI-native startups
- Business strategists navigating AI transformation
- Technical founders who want stronger product and business fluency
- Career professionals transitioning into AI product roles

### What This Track Prepares You to Do

Graduates of this track can:

- Lead the full product lifecycle for AI-powered products — from discovery through launch and iteration
- Evaluate, integrate, and manage AI/ML capabilities within a product strategy
- Develop and defend a business strategy grounded in market analysis, competitive dynamics, and financial modeling
- Run rigorous user research and translate findings into prioritized product decisions
- Communicate product vision and strategy across engineering, design, sales, and executive teams
- Design and execute go-to-market plans, including positioning, launch sequencing, and post-launch learning loops

### Track Structure

| Component | Count |
|---|---|
| Core Foundation domains (required for all tracks) | 6 |
| Specialization domains (required for this track) | 8 |
| Elective domains (choose 2 from 4 options) | 2 |
| **Total specialization competencies** | **10** |

The Core Foundations are defined in [FRAMEWORK.md §5](../../FRAMEWORK.md#5-core-foundations). This document covers the specialization and elective domains only.

## 3. Required Domain 1 — Product Strategy & Roadmapping

### Competency Standard

The learner can define and communicate a product vision, develop a coherent product strategy linked to business outcomes, create and prioritize a roadmap against competing constraints, and articulate tradeoff decisions to diverse stakeholders with clarity and confidence.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Writes a one-page product strategy document that connects a specific customer problem to a defensible business outcome
- Builds a prioritized roadmap using a structured framework (RICE, ICE, MoSCoW, or equivalent) and explains the tradeoffs made
- Articulates why items were deprioritized — not just what is on the roadmap
- Responds to a roadmap challenge from engineering, sales, or leadership without capitulating or dismissing the concern
- Connects roadmap decisions to measurable business metrics

## 4. Required Domain 2 — AI/ML Product Design & Evaluation

### Competency Standard

The learner understands how machine learning systems work at the conceptual level, can evaluate AI capabilities and limitations critically, design product experiences that account for AI behavior (including failure modes), and assess the ethical dimensions of deploying AI in a product context.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Explains the difference between supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning and gives a product example for each
- Describes what training data, model evaluation metrics (precision/recall, F1, AUC), and inference latency mean — and why each matters to a product team
- Designs a product feature that degrades gracefully when the model is wrong
- Conducts an AI risk assessment identifying potential failure modes, edge cases, and user harm scenarios
- Writes a product spec for an AI feature that specifies model behavior expectations, evaluation criteria, and fallback logic

## 5. Required Domain 3 — Business Strategy & Competitive Analysis

### Competency Standard

The learner can analyze an industry's competitive structure, identify a company's strategic position and sources of competitive advantage, evaluate strategic options using established frameworks, and produce a defensible strategic recommendation grounded in evidence.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Applies Porter's Five Forces or a comparable framework to a real industry and draws conclusions about attractiveness and dynamics
- Identifies a company's source of competitive advantage and explains whether it is durable
- Produces a competitive analysis with at least 5 competitors, comparing on dimensions relevant to the strategic question
- Writes a strategic recommendation (1,500+ words) with a clear thesis, evidence, and consideration of alternative paths
- Reads and critiques a published business strategy or analyst report

## 6. Required Domain 4 — Financial Analysis & Business Modeling

### Competency Standard

The learner can read and interpret financial statements, build and evaluate financial models for business decisions, and apply financial reasoning to product and strategy questions — including unit economics, pricing, and investment tradeoffs.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Reads a P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statement and identifies the key performance signals
- Builds a three-statement financial model for a hypothetical business scenario (revenue, cost, profit)
- Calculates and interprets unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC ratio, payback period, gross margin
- Evaluates a pricing decision using cost-plus, value-based, and competitive benchmarking approaches
- Reads and critiques the financial assumptions in a business plan or pitch deck

## 7. Required Domain 5 — Data Analysis for Product Decisions

### Competency Standard

The learner can define and measure product metrics, design and interpret A/B experiments, identify and avoid common analytical pitfalls, and use data to drive product decisions — including knowing when data is insufficient and judgment is required.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Defines a north star metric, supporting metrics, and guardrail metrics for a real product
- Designs an A/B test including hypothesis, sample size calculation, success criteria, and guardrail conditions
- Identifies at least three common analytical errors in a given analysis (selection bias, multiple comparisons, survivorship bias, etc.)
- Writes an analysis memo translating a data finding into a product recommendation
- Distinguishes between correlation and causation in a product data context and explains why it matters

## 8. Required Domain 6 — User Research & Product Discovery

### Competency Standard

The learner can plan and conduct user research using appropriate qualitative and quantitative methods, synthesize findings into actionable product insights, and connect research to product decisions with documented reasoning.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Writes a research plan including research questions, method selection rationale, participant criteria, and analysis approach
- Conducts at least 5 user interviews and synthesizes them using affinity mapping or an equivalent structured method
- Selects the appropriate research method for a given product question (when to use interviews vs. surveys vs. usability testing vs. analytics)
- Writes a research readout that distinguishes observations from interpretations and connects findings to product decisions
- Distinguishes between stated and revealed preferences in user research and explains the difference

## 9. Required Domain 7 — Stakeholder Management & Cross-Functional Leadership

### Competency Standard

The learner can map stakeholders, manage competing interests across functions (engineering, design, sales, marketing, leadership, legal), align teams behind a shared direction, and navigate disagreement productively — especially without direct authority.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Produces a stakeholder map for a real product decision identifying key players, their interests, and their influence
- Describes a specific situation where they navigated a conflict between two functions and explains what they did and why
- Writes a communication to engineering, sales, or leadership about a difficult tradeoff — explaining the decision without oversimplifying
- Identifies when escalation is appropriate and what should and should not be escalated
- Demonstrates ability to maintain momentum without consensus (decide and explain vs. wait for unanimity)

## 10. Required Domain 8 — Go-to-Market Strategy & Product Launch

### Competency Standard

The learner can develop a go-to-market strategy for a product or feature, including target customer definition, positioning, channel strategy, launch sequencing, and success metrics — and can evaluate the success of a past launch with structured analysis.

### What "Competent" Looks Like

- Writes an ICP (ideal customer profile) with specific, falsifiable criteria — not vague demographic descriptors
- Develops a positioning statement using a structured framework (Geoffrey Moore's, Jobs-to-be-Done, or equivalent)
- Produces a launch plan with defined phases, channel selection rationale, and success/failure criteria
- Conducts a post-launch retrospective on a real launch: what worked, what didn't, what would be done differently
- Distinguishes between product marketing, sales enablement, and demand generation — and explains when each is the bottleneck

### Elective A — Entrepreneurship & Venture Building

**Competency Standard**

The learner understands the lifecycle of a new venture from idea to early traction, can evaluate startup ideas using structured frameworks, and demonstrates understanding of venture economics, investor dynamics, and the operational challenges of early-stage company building.

**What "Competent" Looks Like**
- Identifies the key assumptions underlying a startup idea and designs experiments to test them cheaply
- Explains the mechanics of a venture financing round: pre-money valuation, dilution, term sheet basics, investor rights
- Writes a lean canvas or equivalent for a real startup idea with specific, testable hypotheses
- Articulates the difference between a product/market fit problem and a go-to-market problem

### Elective B — B2B Sales & Revenue Operations

**Competency Standard**

The learner understands the full B2B sales cycle from lead generation through close, can evaluate a revenue operation's structure and efficiency, and can collaborate productively with sales teams as a PM — including pricing, packaging, and enterprise deal support.

**What "Competent" Looks Like**
- Maps the stages of a B2B sales cycle and explains what happens at each stage
- Explains the role of SDRs, AEs, and SEs — and how a PM interfaces with each
- Calculates and interprets pipeline health metrics: conversion rates, cycle time, win rate, average deal size
- Explains the difference between volume-based and value-based pricing and when each applies

### Elective C — Technical Architecture for Product Managers

**Competency Standard**

The learner understands the key architectural concepts underlying modern software systems, can communicate effectively with engineering teams about technical constraints, and can evaluate the product implications of technical decisions — including performance, scalability, and security.

**What "Competent" Looks Like**
- Explains the difference between monolithic and microservices architectures and their product tradeoffs
- Reads a basic system architecture diagram and asks informed questions about bottlenecks, single points of failure, and scaling limits
- Explains what an API is and how REST, GraphQL, and webhooks differ — with product examples
- Articulates how a PM should think about technical debt: when it matters to the user and when it doesn't
