## 1. What Peer Reviewers Do

ODF peer reviewers evaluate two types of submissions:

**Capstone Projects**
The capstone is the integrative, public, peer-reviewed project required for every ODF credential. Two reviewers evaluate each capstone against a defined rubric. Your assessment becomes part of the learner's permanent CLR — visible to any employer who reads their credential.

**PLA Claims (as needed)**
Complex prior learning claims may be flagged for peer review. This is optional and at the learner's request or the maintainer's recommendation. Reviewers evaluate whether the evidence clearly maps to the competency standard.

**Time commitment:** 1–3 reviews per quarter. Each capstone review takes approximately 2–4 hours (reading the capstone + completing the rubric + writing feedback).

## 2. Eligibility

**For capstone review:**
- 5+ years of relevant professional experience in the ODF track you're reviewing, OR
- Demonstrated academic expertise in a relevant domain (doctoral work, published research, professional credentials)
- Willingness to provide structured, rubric-based feedback within 30 days of assignment
- Ability to evaluate work fairly and without personal bias toward the learner

**For PLA review:**
- 3+ years of professional experience in the specific competency domain being claimed
- Ability to evaluate whether a prior learning claim maps credibly to a competency standard

**Not eligible if:**
- You have a direct personal or professional relationship with the learner (friend, colleague, employee, employer, family member)
- You have a financial interest in the learner's outcome
- You are completing the ODF yourself and reviewing work in a domain you have claimed or are claiming

If you're unsure whether you have a conflict, disclose it and let the maintainers decide.

## 3. How to Sign Up

Open a GitHub issue with the label `volunteer-reviewer`.

Include:
- **Your professional background** (2–3 sentences)
- **The track(s) you can review** (Business + AI Product, Computer Science, etc.)
- **Domain expertise** — which specific competency domains you're most qualified to evaluate
- **Your availability** — roughly how many reviews per quarter you can commit to

A maintainer will follow up within 14 days to complete onboarding. Onboarding takes about 30 minutes and covers:
- The capstone rubric and what each score means
- The review process and timeline expectations
- The conflict of interest policy
- How reviews are logged in the CLR

## 4. The Capstone Review Process

### Step 1 — Submission

The learner submits their capstone via a GitHub issue with label `capstone-review`. The submission includes:
- Link to the public capstone
- Link to the learner's degree plan
- List of competency domains the capstone covers
- Written reflection (500–1,000 words) on what was learned

### Step 2 — Reviewer Assignment

Maintainers assign two reviewers from the active reviewer pool. Reviewers must:
- Have relevant domain expertise (at minimum in one of the claimed competency domains)
- Have no conflict of interest with the learner
- Confirm availability to complete the review within 30 days

If you are assigned and cannot complete the review within 30 days, notify the maintainers immediately so a replacement can be arranged.

### Step 3 — Independent Review

Each reviewer evaluates the capstone independently, without consulting the other reviewer. Use the rubric in Section 5. Complete the review template in Section 6.

**What to evaluate:**
- The capstone itself (the public artifact)
- The learner's written reflection
- Whether the claimed competency domains are actually demonstrated
- Whether the evidence of learning is credible

**What not to evaluate:**
- How impressive the topic is
- Whether you agree with the learner's conclusions
- The learner's prior credentials or background (which you may know from their degree plan)
- Any factor not in the rubric

### Step 4 — Submitting Your Review

Post your completed review template as a comment on the learner's GitHub issue. Include:
- Your rubric scores (one score per dimension)
- Your written feedback (see template)
- Your declaration that you have no conflict of interest

### Step 5 — Decision

| Outcome | Condition |
|---|---|
| **Pass** | Average score ≥ 3.0 across both reviewers, no dimension below 2 for either reviewer |
| **Revise and Resubmit** | Average score ≥ 2.0 but below 3.0, or at least one dimension below 2 |
| **Fail** | Average score below 2.0 |

The learner may revise and resubmit once if the initial decision is Revise and Resubmit. A second review is conducted by the same two reviewers if available, or new reviewers if not.

Reviewer names and scores are logged in the learner's CLR.

## 5. The Capstone Rubric

*From [FRAMEWORK.md §7](../FRAMEWORK.md#7-capstone-project)*

| Dimension | 1 — Insufficient | 2 — Developing | 3 — Competent | 4 — Exemplary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Competency Integration** | Single domain only | 2 domains referenced | 3+ domains clearly applied | Sophisticated multi-domain synthesis |
| **Evidence of Learning** | Assertion only | Some documentation | Clear evidence trail | Comprehensive, detailed documentation |
| **Public Accessibility** | Not public | Partially accessible | Fully accessible | Well-organized and navigable |
| **Written Reflection** | Absent or superficial | Describes what was done | Connects work to framework | Insightful, self-critical, forward-looking |
| **Rigor** | Low effort, incomplete | Moderate effort | Substantial effort and quality | Exceptional quality |

**Passing standard:** Average score of 3.0 or higher across all dimensions, with no dimension below 2.

### Scoring Guidance

**Competency Integration**
- Score 1: The project only demonstrates one competency domain
- Score 2: Two domains are mentioned but the connection is thin
- Score 3: Three or more domains are explicitly applied and the connections are clear
- Score 4: The integration is seamless — the project wouldn't make sense without all the domains

**Evidence of Learning**
- Score 1: The learner asserts they learned things; no documentation
- Score 2: Some work products exist but the learning trail is patchy
- Score 3: You can trace what was done, why, and what was learned
- Score 4: The documentation is so thorough it could be used to replicate or extend the work

**Public Accessibility**
- Score 1: The capstone is not publicly accessible
- Score 2: It's technically accessible but hard to navigate
- Score 3: A stranger could find and read it without help
- Score 4: It's organized, readable, and would benefit someone who found it independently

**Written Reflection**
- Score 1: No reflection, or a paragraph that says "I learned a lot"
- Score 2: Describes what happened but doesn't connect to the framework
- Score 3: Explicitly connects the work to the competency standards; shows self-awareness
- Score 4: Honest about limitations, identifies what they'd do differently, forward-looking

**Rigor**
- Score 1: The work is incomplete, shallow, or clearly took fewer than 20 hours
- Score 2: Moderate effort; gaps are visible
- Score 3: Substantial effort, quality product
- Score 4: The kind of work you'd be proud to share publicly and that would hold up to expert scrutiny

## 7. PLA Review Process

Prior learning claims are usually self-assessed by the learner and documented in their degree plan. Peer review of PLA claims is triggered when:
- The learner requests it (for complex or high-stakes claims)
- A maintainer flags a claim as requiring additional validation
- An appeal is filed on a rejected claim

**What PLA reviewers evaluate:**
- Does the evidence exist and is it verifiable?
- Does the evidence plausibly map to the competency standard?
- Is the written reflection credible and specific?
- Does the claimed tier match the evidence quality?

**What PLA reviewers do not evaluate:**
- Whether the learner is impressive or likable
- Whether the professional field the evidence comes from is prestigious
- Whether they would have made the same career choices

**PLA review timeline:** 14 days from assignment.

**PLA review output:** A brief written assessment (200–400 words) posted to the relevant GitHub issue. PLA reviewers make a recommendation (accept, accept with modification, reject with rationale); the final decision is made by the maintainers.

## 8. Recognition

**What reviewers receive for each capstone reviewed:**
- Named credit as a reviewer in the learner's Comprehensive Learner Record (permanent and public)
- Listing in the ODF community reviewer directory
- Credit in the annual ODF project report

**Over time:** As the reviewer community grows, it will develop a track record. Reviewers who consistently produce high-quality, fair, specific feedback will be recognized as senior reviewers. Senior reviewers will play a role in shaping the rubric and review standards as the framework evolves.

## 9. FAQ

**How much time does a capstone review take?**
Approximately 2–4 hours: reading the capstone (30–60 min), reviewing the reflection, completing the rubric, and writing feedback. If the capstone is complex or the competency mapping requires careful evaluation, it may take longer.

**What if I disagree with the other reviewer's score?**
Reviewers evaluate independently. If your scores differ significantly (2+ points on any dimension), the maintainers may ask both reviewers to discuss and see if they can converge. If not, both scores are logged and the average is used. Neither reviewer is wrong — rubrics have inherent subjectivity.

**What if I think the learner cheated or fabricated evidence?**
Contact a maintainer privately via a GitHub issue labeled `integrity-concern`. Do not post your concern publicly. Maintainers will investigate before any decision is made.

**What if I think the rubric is wrong?**
Open a GitHub issue with label `framework-change`. Reviewers are well-positioned to improve the rubric — that feedback is genuinely valuable.

**Can I review a capstone from someone I know professionally?**
Only if the professional relationship is distant (e.g., you work in the same broad industry but have never worked together, never had a financial relationship, and have no stake in their outcome). If in doubt, disclose and let the maintainers decide.

**What if the learner disputes my review?**
The appeals process is defined in [FRAMEWORK.md §9](../FRAMEWORK.md#9-governance--appeals). Disputes about whether a review was conducted correctly (not just disagreement with scores) are handled by a fresh review panel. You are not required to participate in appeals.
